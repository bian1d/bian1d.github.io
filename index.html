Research & Projects

A few things I've worked on, most recent first. Highlighted: the robot demo and my first paper.

Motion Planning and Control for a Collaborative Robot–Quadruped System
Laboratory of Prof. He Wang, Peking University
Mar–Jun 2025
demo video (with sound) / on-site photo

RRT-based collaborative motion planning for a robot arm (GalBot) and a quadruped (Unitree Go2), so the two execute tasks in sync without colliding. Calibrated the quadruped's trajectory and locomotion parameters to close the sim-to-real gap on the real platforms.

Interpretable Data Attribution for LLM Post-training
Prof. Liangming Pan's group, Peking University
Dec 2025 – present
builds on Grosse et al. 2023 / Anthropic research page

Which RLHF training samples make a language model less toxic? I apply influence-function data attribution to PPO rollouts, a setting that prior (pretraining-only) attribution work did not cover. EK-FAC influence functions implemented from scratch in PyTorch (within 1e-3 of brute-force Fisher computation); now extending the method to RLVR-trained reasoning models. Thumbnail: token-wise influence heatmap from Grosse et al.

Molecular Force Field Prediction with Graph Neural Networks
Laboratory of Prof. Di He, Peking University
Jun – Nov 2024
OC20 dataset / EquiformerV2 / animation: Open Catalyst Project

Does physics-informed design make force prediction more accurate? Forces are computed as energy gradients with respect to atomic positions, and the model is trained on OC20's structure-to-energy-and-forces task (~1.28M adsorbate–catalyst systems, like the adsorbate relaxing onto a metal surface on the left), matching the state-of-the-art EquiformerV2. I also wired real-time data streaming into Meta's Fairseq so training stayed stable across 8× RTX 4090 GPUs.

Large-Scale Contextual Market Equilibrium Computation through Deep Learning
Yunxuan Ma, Yide Bian, Hao Xu, Weitao Yang, Jingshu Zhao, Zhijian Duan, Feng Wang, Xiaotie Deng
Frontiers of Algorithmics (IJTCS-FAW 2025), LNCS, Springer, pp. 356–371
arXiv / Springer / abstract

MarketFCNet approximates market equilibrium for markets with millions of buyers by parameterizing each allocation with a network that only sees the buyer's and the good's contexts. We also propose Nash Gap, a metric for how far an allocation–price pair is from equilibrium.
